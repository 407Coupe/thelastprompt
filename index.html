The evening

First and foremost, it is a proper murder mystery.

The Last Prompt is played around a real dinner table, with real people, real conversation and tangible evidence. The technology supports the mystery. It never replaces the human drama.

01

You become the cast

Guests can play themselves, an alter ego, or a fantasy version of themselves. Each person receives a role, secrets, relationships and private objectives woven into the story.

02

Clues arrive in the room

Printed correspondence, photographs, keycards, notes, objects, sealed envelopes and other physical material appear throughout the evening. Some clues are public. Others belong to one person alone.

03

You investigate face to face

The heart of the experience is conversation: questioning, bargaining, lying, accusing and deciding who to believe while dinner continues around you.

Physical by design

Not an app. Not a video game. Not a night spent staring at a screen.

Phones are used sparingly and with purpose. Most of the evening happens in the room, around the table, between the guests.

What is physically in the room

Each event is staged with material that gives the mystery weight and texture.

Character dossiers and private instructions
Sealed evidence envelopes
Printed emails, reports, photographs and handwritten notes
Objects, keycards and other physical clues
Timed evidence releases during dinner
A final accusation and reveal
The AI layer

The AI is part of the mystery, not the whole mystery.

During the investigation, guests can consult VERITY: a controlled generative AI system with access to the fictional company's records. It can answer questions, search the case material, compare evidence and help you build a theory.

VERITY is central to the investigation. The questions you ask matter. So does how you ask them, what you follow up on, what evidence you request, and how readily you accept an answer. Used well, VERITY can be extraordinarily helpful. Used carelessly, it can send your investigation in the wrong direction.

By the end of the evening, you will almost certainly have discovered a few things about getting better answers from generative AI, checking what sits behind an answer, and deciding when AI deserves your trust.

VERITY // INCIDENT MODE

Q: Where was Daniel Mercer at 8:47pm?
A: Access logs place Daniel Mercer near the server room shortly before 8:47pm. Would you like me to show the underlying record, compare this with witness accounts, or identify conflicting evidence?
ASK BETTER QUESTIONS

VERITY can search records, compare sources, test a theory and surface evidence. What you discover depends partly on what you ask.

There is another layer

You may leave better at using AI than when you arrived.

The evening is not disguised training. The murder mystery has to work as entertainment in its own right.

But solving it rewards many of the same habits that make generative AI useful in real life: asking precise questions, challenging assumptions, requesting sources, following up when an answer is vague, comparing conflicting information and knowing when to go back to the original evidence.

For corporate groups, that creates an unusual bonus. People learn by trying to solve the case, not by sitting through a seminar.
