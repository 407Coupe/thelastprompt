The evening

First and foremost, it is a proper investigation.

The Last Prompt is played around a real dinner table, with real people, real argument and tangible evidence. Your guests are not acting out suspects. They are the detectives trying to reconstruct a murder that has already happened. The technology supports the investigation. It never replaces the human drama.

01

You become the detectives

Each table becomes a detective agency. The suspects are fictional characters revealed through statements, documents, records and performed testimony. Your job is to decide which suspect killed the victim, how, and why.

02

Evidence arrives in the room

Printed correspondence, photographs, keycards, notes, objects, sealed envelopes and performed testimony appear throughout the evening. Some evidence is shared. Private tip-offs preserve information asymmetry between individual detectives.

03

You investigate face to face

The heart of the experience is the table: comparing evidence, challenging theories, deciding what to share, questioning witnesses and working out which question to ask VERITY next.

Physical by design

Not an app. Not a video game. Not a night spent staring at a screen.

Phones are used sparingly and with purpose. Most of the evening happens in the room, around the table, between the guests.

Picture the room

One scene from the evening.

Dinner is underway when every table receives a sealed evidence envelope marked OPEN NOW.

Inside is a printed access log, a handwritten note and a photograph. One detective has also received a private tip-off on their phone that changes the meaning of the note — but nobody else at the table knows that yet.

At the same moment, VERITY becomes available on each table's phones. One group asks, “Who had access to the server room?” Another asks, “Show us the evidence that places anyone near the server room between 8:40 and 8:50.”

The two tables get very different levels of insight.

The point is not the screen.

The energy is in the room: detectives comparing the printed evidence, challenging each other's theories, deciding whether to reveal private information, and working out which question to ask VERITY next.

The AI layer

VERITY is a witness. A powerful one. Not a neutral one.

During the investigation, detectives can question VERITY: a controlled generative AI system with access to the fictional company's records. It can search, compare, summarise, reason across evidence and respond to whatever your table decides to ask.

But VERITY has already been touched by the events inside the story. Its answers are fluent. Its confidence is convincing. Its version of the truth may not always deserve yours.

The tables that ask precise questions, request the underlying record, test alternative explanations and verify what VERITY tells them are the tables most likely to crack the case. Without turning the evening into training, the investigation rewards exactly the habits that make generative AI more useful in real life.

VERITY // INCIDENT MODE
CASE ACCESS ACTIVE
Q: Where was Daniel Mercer at 8:47pm?
A: Access logs place Daniel Mercer near the server room shortly before 8:47pm. Would you like me to show the underlying record, compare this with witness accounts, or identify conflicting evidence?
ASK BETTER QUESTIONS

VERITY can search records, compare sources, test a theory and surface evidence. What you discover depends partly on what you ask.

A hosted experience

You do not have to run anything.

This is not a box of materials dropped on a table. Matt hosts the evening in person, allowing every guest to stay inside the story.

The result is closer to private immersive theatre than a conventional party game: carefully paced, highly social and tailored to the people in the room.

The experience can be hosted at a hotel, restaurant, conference venue, retreat, corporate venue or other suitable event location anywhere in New Zealand.

Three formats. One investigation engine.

From one executive table to a room of four hundred.

Because the guests are detectives rather than cast members, the same carefully engineered case can scale without losing the core experience. Every table works the evidence, interrogates VERITY and commits to a verdict before the reveal.

The Dinner

8–10 detectives

The flagship format. One table, hosted end to end over approximately three hours. Intimate, competitive and rich in discussion, with private tip-offs preventing the group from collapsing into one shared view.

The Conference

40–400 guests

Each table becomes a rival detective agency working the same case. Evidence drops and testimony are staged across the room, every table interrogates VERITY, and the final reveal shows who solved the murder — and who trusted the machine too readily.

The Commission

One client. One night.

A premium mystery created once for a specific organisation. The fictional company can echo the client's world, with bespoke suspects, documents and industry detail. It is performed for that client and then retired.

There is another layer

The case quietly rewards better AI judgement.

The evening is not disguised training. The investigation has to work as entertainment in its own right.

But solving it rewards many of the same habits that make generative AI useful in real life: asking precise questions, challenging confident answers, requesting sources, testing alternative explanations, comparing conflicting information and knowing when to return to primary evidence.

That creates an unusual corporate benefit: people learn by trying to solve the case, not by sitting through a seminar.
